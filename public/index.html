Tacho Downloads

If You Have Not Downloaded
The card only holds about 28 days, so anything older than that can be overwritten and lost for good. If you are overdue, tell your transport manager or operator now and get the card downloaded at the next chance. Do not delete, alter or swap anything to cover the gap. If days are already lost, keep any printouts and make a written note of the missing days and why, and give it to your operator. Gaps in your records are what DVSA looks for at a check.
Your Obligation as a Driver
Carry your records for the current day and the previous 28 days (56 days if you have made an international journey to or from the EU), hand them over when an enforcement officer asks, let your operator download your card, and report a lost, damaged or faulty card straight away. Downloading the card on schedule is the operator duty, but you need to make the card available.
Why Download the Data
Your driver card holds about 28 days of data. After that the oldest data is overwritten and lost for good, so a missed download can leave a gap you cannot fill. The data is also your proof that you followed the hours rules. An enforcement officer can check the current day and the previous 28 days, and DVSA can fine driver hours offences found in that period.
Law
The operator must download driver cards at least every 28 days and the vehicle unit at least every 90 days, and keep the data for at least a year. As a driver you must carry your records for the current day and the previous 28 days, hand them over when asked, and let the operator download your card. If you employ yourself as an owner driver, the operator duties are yours.
Company Policy
Anything stricter than the law, such as downloading weekly, handing your card in on set days, or checking your own cards. It is not the law, but your employer can act on it under your contract, for example with a disciplinary.
Penalties in Court
Failing to use a tachograph, failing to install one, or failing to hand over records when an enforcement officer asks: a Level 5 fine, which is unlimited in England and Wales.
Failing to make or keep records: a Level 4 fine, up to £2,500.
Making a false entry or altering a record, or the tachograph seal, with intent to deceive: a Level 5 fine, and up to 2 years in prison if tried in the Crown Court.
Penalties at the Roadside
DVSA can issue fixed penalties of £50 to £300 each for hours and tachograph offences, up to 5 at one check, including offences from the last 28 days. Unpaid ones go up by 50% in England and Wales. DVSA can also prohibit you from driving, immobilise the vehicle, or refer you or your operator to the Traffic Commissioner, who looks at whether you are fit to hold your licence.
Points
Fixed penalties for hours and tachograph offences are non-endorsable, so they carry no points. Points apply to roadworthiness offences, such as 3 points for defective brakes on a goods vehicle.

Sign Shapes and Colours

Circles give orders. A red ring mostly means you must not do something (a prohibition, such as a speed limit or no entry). A solid blue circle mostly tells you what you must do (such as turn left or keep left).

Triangles warn you of a hazard ahead, such as a bend, a junction, animals or a slippery road.

Rectangles give information or directions. All information signs are rectangles.

Plates under a sign add detail, such as a distance, a time or a vehicle type.

Direction sign colours: blue is a motorway, green is a primary route, white with a black border is a local or non-primary route, and brown is a tourist attraction. An aircraft symbol shows the way to an airport.

Roadworks signs warn of works and temporary changes, such as lane closures and temporary speed limits.

In Wales many signs are in Welsh and English. Some older designs are still on the road. Source: the Highway Code, Traffic signs section on gov.uk.

A quick reference for goods vehicle drivers in Great Britain, checked against the DVSA guidance on gov.uk (last updated 1 July 2026). The Today tab follows these rules. Always check gov.uk for changes.

Which Rules Apply
Assimilated (formerly EU) rules apply to most goods vehicles over 3.5 tonnes, and to international and AETR journeys. GB domestic rules apply to some smaller or exempt vehicles that stay in Great Britain. Which one applies depends on the vehicle's size, construction and use, not how it is registered. The rest of this app uses the assimilated rules.
Driving Limits
Daily driving: 9 hours, up to 10 hours twice in a fixed week. Weekly: 56 hours. Any two weeks in a row: 90 hours. A fixed week runs from 00:00 Monday to 24:00 Sunday.
Breaks
After 4h 30m of driving take 45 minutes, or 15 minutes then 30 minutes. A break under 15 minutes does not count. Other work and POA are not breaks.
Daily Rest
Regular daily rest is 11 continuous hours within 24 hours of the end of your last rest. It can be split into 3 hours then 9 hours (12 in total). A reduced daily rest is at least 9 hours and under 11, allowed up to 3 times between weekly rests.
Weekly Rest
Regular weekly rest is at least 45 continuous hours. A reduced weekly rest is at least 24 hours and under 45, and must be made up by an equal rest taken in one block before the end of the third week after, attached to a rest of at least 9 hours. In any two fixed weeks you need two regular weekly rests, or one regular and one reduced. The next weekly rest must start within 6 days (144 hours) of the end of the last one.
Ferry or Train
A regular daily rest on a ferry or train can be interrupted up to twice, for no more than 1 hour in total. A regular weekly rest can only be interrupted on a leg of at least 8 hours with a sleeper cabin or couchette.
Two Drivers (Multi-Manning)
The second driver does not have to drive for the first hour, then must. Each driver needs 9 hours of rest within 30 hours of their last daily or weekly rest. Together they can drive up to 20 hours before a daily rest, if both are entitled to 10-hour days.
Unforeseen Events
To reach a suitable stopping place, home or base, you may go over the daily and weekly driving limits by up to 1 hour, or 2 hours before a regular weekly rest after a 30 minute break. Write down why on the tachograph, and the extra must be made up by the end of the third week.
Working Time (WTR 2005)
Average 48 hours a week over 17 weeks (26 weeks with a collective or workforce agreement), up to 60 hours in any one week, with no opt-out. No more than 6 hours of work without a break: 30 minutes for 6 to 9 hours, 45 minutes for more than 9 hours, in parts of at least 15 minutes. If you work at night (00:00 to 04:00) you must not work more than 10 hours in any 24. POA is known in advance and is not working time. Employers keep records for 2 years.
GB Domestic Rules
Up to 10 hours driving and 11 hours on duty in a working day, with no set break or rest times but your employer must make sure you get adequate rest. Exempt from the daily duty limit on days you do not drive. You need a written weekly record sheet if your vehicle needs an operator licence and you drive more than 4 hours in a day, or a tachograph instead. These rules only apply in Great Britain.
Tachograph Records
At the roadside you must produce records for today and the previous 28 days, or 56 days if you made an international journey to or from the EU. Digital drivers need their own card and enough printer paper. Driver cards must be downloaded every 28 days and the vehicle unit every 90 days. Analogue charts go back to the operator within 42 days.
Lost, Stolen or Faulty Card
You may drive without it for up to 15 calendar days, using two printouts a day (start and end), with no change of vehicle. Report it to DVLA and apply for a new card within 7 calendar days. A forgotten card cannot be replaced by manual records.
Penalties
See the Tacho tab and the info buttons on the Today tab for fixed penalties and court fines. Fixed penalties are graduated by seriousness. If you have no UK address you may have to pay a deposit on the spot and not drive on until you do.

General tips for a newly qualified driver. These are not official guidance, so check gov.uk for the detail.

Before Your First Day
Have your licence, Driver CPC card and digital tachograph card with you. Ask for a familiarisation drive and a handover on the vehicle's controls, tail lift or fridge unit. Check who to ring if you break down or are running late.
Every Day
Do the walkaround check before the first drive, using the Walk tab, and report defects before you move. Put your mode on the tachograph correctly: driving, other work, POA or rest. Plan your breaks around the 4h 30m limit rather than leaving it late.
Staying Legal
Keep a note of how many 10-hour days you have used this week. Make sure your card is downloaded on time and record anything unusual, such as a ferry or an unforeseen delay, on a printout or manual entry.
Asking for Help
If you are unsure about a rule, ask your transport manager before you drive. It is better to stop and ask than to break the rules.
